\begin{abstract}
We specify an auditable program for training the best protein embedding model
obtainable under a fixed four-accelerator budget. The system reproduces the
released ESMC 300M and 600M architectures, draws from verified UniRef90,
MGnify, and OMG/IMG cluster representatives, uses a two-stage context and
source schedule, and separates a fast checkpoint diagnostic from release-only
evaluation. Two ESMC-300M Stage-1 runs now provide a local scale check on the
same homology-screened 9M-protein pool: a four-hour run reached 20,971 steps
and 1.279 billion filled residues, while a fresh sixteen-hour run completed
84,000 steps and 5.123 billion residues. The trusted four-task P-CORE-Q4 rose
from 25.461 to 30.661 and full 20,775-chain contact P@L rose from 0.1013 to
0.1636. Both remain below the released ESMC-300M references of 37.485 and
0.5387, respectively, so the result establishes useful training-scale
progress but not a quality promotion. The new source-qualified
v0.5-alpha panel completes on released
ESMC-300M/600M/6B, but zero of five runnable tasks is strictly scale-monotone;
only MegaScale establishes a paired 6B-over-300M gain, so no new aggregate or
model ranking is issued. This two-page main report
fixes the decision protocol and immediate work; the appendices provide full
data lineage, cleaning and selection, training defaults, benchmark
construction, uncertainty, three-scale ESMC baselines, and release gates.
Every Python environment is created and locked with \texttt{uv}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Objective and experimental contract}

The objective is not merely lower masked-language-model (MLM) loss. It is a
better \emph{frozen sequence representation}: useful across evolution,
structure, function, localization, interaction, and fitness under low-capacity
probes. The present selection core uses only the four tasks whose reconstructed
benchmarks discriminate the known scale ladder; function/EC and interaction/PPI
remain reported diagnostics. We use ESMC because its released family offers a clean scale ladder and
strong contact representations \citep{candido2026language}. The working system
is deliberately small in surface area, inspired by nanochat's inspectable
organization \citep{karpathy2025nanochat}, while the scientific claim is bound
to data and evaluation receipts rather than repository simplicity.

Claims have three levels. A \emph{diagnostic} guides engineering but has no
headline aggregate. A \emph{candidate comparison} requires equal compute,
identical manifests, at least two seeds, and paired uncertainty. A
\emph{release claim} additionally requires homology decontamination, exact
all six raw probes, P-CORE-Q4, full contact evaluation, a clean locked rebuild,
and licensing review.
This prevents a fast proxy from quietly becoming the scientific target.

\section{Complete data lineage and selection}
\label{app:data}

\subsection{Source databases and evidence layers}

The ESMC paper trains on UniRef, MGnify, and the Joint Genome Institute (JGI)
metagenome collection, clustered at 70\% identity
\citep{candido2026language,suzek2015uniref,richardson2023mgnify,grigoriev2012jgi}.
Our open reconstruction uses UniRef90 2023\_02, MGnify 2023\_02, and the
IMG/JGI-labelled portion of the OMG payload \citep{cornman2024omg} as the
JGI-role arm.
This substitution is always named \emph{OMG/IMG}; it is not claimed to be the
paper's private JGI corpus.

The production lineage has three tables. Table~\ref{tab:transfer} reports the
full-corpus funnel from original records through quality and source filtering,
global exact deduplication, and source-specific MMseqs2
\citep{steinegger2018linclust} clustering. Table~\ref{tab:speedrun-corpus} is
the large-run planning reservoir over those 70\%-identity representatives; it
is not the corpus materialized for the first local campaign.
Table~\ref{tab:first-corpus} is the verified 9M-protein local pool. The realized
training stream is drawn from that pool using the Stage-1 source mixture. Thus
the local lineage is directly
Table~\ref{tab:transfer} $\rightarrow$ Table~\ref{tab:speedrun-corpus}
$\rightarrow$ Table~\ref{tab:first-corpus} $\rightarrow$ sampled exposures.

Residue compute is a separate accounting layer. The measured four-H100 300M
rates imply 122--130B Stage-1 plus 45--49B Stage-2 filled residues, or 168--178B
total, in the ideal eight-H100 seven-day planning case. The observed mean sampled
lengths (267 residues in Stage 1 and 317 in Stage 2) convert that budget to the
600--639M protein/crop draws in Table~\ref{tab:speedrun-corpus}. The transferred
representative FASTAs contain approximately 183--185B residue characters before
final exclusions, so they can feed the run globally but provide little reserve.
The former 200B figure is a desirable headroom target, not a hard minimum. More
importantly, source ratios force 2.74--2.92 nominal UniRef representative passes
while most MGnify representatives remain unseen. The exact eight-GPU rate and
post-exclusion residue inventory must be measured before fixing the stop count.

At each step, the stage selects a source using Table~\ref{tab:speedrun-corpus},
then draws a representative from that source and a random contiguous crop when
the protein exceeds the context ceiling. Production should use deterministic
shuffled passes within each source and cycle only when required, instead of
independent sampling with replacement that creates avoidable early duplicates.
The realized training corpus is the resulting ordered exposure manifest: source
counts, representative hashes, crop offsets, residues, and repeat counts. The
completed 30-minute pilot used only 250,000 training and 4,096 validation
representatives per source, excluded 29 exact evaluation matches, and processed
449,536 Stage-1 plus 76,928 Stage-2 draws; those pilot counts are not a
production-reservoir recommendation.

\paragraph{Decontamination status.}
The completed 30-minute pilot remains exact-only and must not support a release
claim. Both reported checkpoints and their evaluations use the same
first-campaign pool, which is properly decontaminated against
\emph{every} probe-fit, validation, and test sequence used by P-CORE and
contact evaluation. Its verified MMseqs2 receipt excludes 816,307 UniRef90,
216,915 MGnify, and 562,349 OMG/IMG candidates; the global union is 1,592,566
because a few sequences belong to multiple source arms. The validation/test
subset alone accounts for 1,382,540 exclusions. The maximum accepted hits for
one query are 7,955/2,468/6,846, below the one-million cap.

The post-preparation verifier independently rehashes all token/index files and
reports zero exact/homology intersections and zero train--validation overlap in
each source. It also proves the actual scans (3,696,551/3,236,640/3,486,965
source records) end before the screened boundaries
(5,162,125/5,161,284/5,161,757). The data-manifest and corpus-verification
SHA-256 digests are \code{a96cd2a09f7e...} and \code{0498f6d06f1f...}.
A temporal or family-disjoint test remains untouched by model and evaluator
development.

\paragraph{Release status.}
Raw source files remain in controlled storage. Redistribution of any derived
shard requires a source-by-source legal and license review. Hugging Face should
initially host manifests, scripts, hashes, statistics, permitted small samples,
and model artifacts---not raw databases by default.

\subsection{Systems and environment}

All Python environments are managed only with \texttt{uv}. The project pins
Python 3.11, declares exact dependencies in \code{pyproject.toml}, and commits
\code{uv.lock}. Qualification and training use \code{uv sync --frozen}; run
receipts store the lockfile digest, Git commit and dirty state, Python/Torch/CUDA
versions, GPU model, configuration digest, and data-manifest digest. The
qualified Linux run used Python 3.11.15, Torch 2.13.0+cu126, CUDA 12.6, and four
A100 80GB PCIe GPUs.

The first campaign used a 14,400-second synchronized training-loop cap and
stopped after 14,400.370 measured training seconds and 14,378.765 accumulated
step-compute seconds. The second used a 57,600-second cap, completed normally
after 56,971.268 training seconds and 56,851.623 step-compute seconds, and
wrote checkpoint SHA-256
\path{b00b3d3784b9d08cd960f766441c8fa0a79827b10630aae702dd2c7a8561700c}.
Setup, final checkpoint writing, and evaluation are outside these clocks.
Throughput was 89,691 and 90,869 model tokens/s, respectively. Duration remains
a systems guardrail, not the scientific unit in Table~\ref{tab:stages}.

Prefix-padded batches are compacted once around the entire transformer. The
in-tree variable-length FlashAttention kernel receives cumulative lengths, so
attention, norms, and MLPs avoid pad rows. Dynamic \code{torch.compile} was
rejected after an AOTAutograd metadata failure before step one. Eager execution
is the canonical path until a compiled graph passes parity at the 512-residue
context. DDP max-reduces elapsed wall time at loop boundaries so every rank
enforces the same cap, and separately max-reduces step compute time for
throughput accounting; the optimizer-step counter controls normal completion.

\section{Evaluation construction, provenance, and baselines}
\label{app:evaluation}

\subsection{Three operating levels}

Every checkpoint receives held-out sequence-mean MLM NLL. Routine speedruns
add a bounded two-task trusted representation vector and a small contact subset; this
vector has \emph{no aggregate}. The final checkpoint of the first campaign
received the complete report: held-out MLM NLL/perplexity; all six raw probe
metrics with 10,000-replicate uncertainty and the four-task selection aggregate;
full 20,775-chain contact P@L with 5,000-replicate uncertainty; plus throughput,
peak memory, filled residues, and source exposures. The merged evaluation
receipt has SHA-256 \code{d21305ceac2c...} and binds checkpoint
\code{e3debec2e01b...}. The released
ESMC-300M reference in Tables~\ref{tab:three-scale-pcore} and
\ref{tab:contact-compare} is the paired baseline. EC and Human PPI remain
visible but cannot select a model while quarantined. P-CORE reuses public task data from
TAPE/PEER, DeepLoc2, and FLIP2
\citep{rao2019tape,xu2022peer,thumuluri2022deeploc2,didi2026flip2}, but freezes
its own embedding, probe, split, metric, normalization, and bootstrap contract.
The machine-readable split ledger has SHA-256
\code{bc28d479a696...}. Its all-split union contains 116,841 unique sequences
(35,467,998 residues); validation/test roles contain 70,305 unique sequences
(24,497,158 residues). ``Train'' below always means probe-fit data, never
language-model pretraining data. DeepLoc2 rotates each of five partitions
through test and the following partition through validation, so every one of
its 28,303 proteins is test exactly once.

For each task $t$, raw metric $m_t$ is converted to an unclipped null-anchored
skill
\[
  s_t = \frac{m_t-n_t}{c_t-n_t},
\]
where $n_t$ is the frozen chance/prevalence null and $c_t=1$ is the fixed
ceiling. All six raw metrics and skills are published. The selection set is
$Q=\{$remote homology, secondary structure, DeepLoc2, FLIP2$\}$, one task in
each of four trusted domains, and
\[
  \mathrm{P\mbox{-}CORE\mbox{-}Q4}
  =100\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{4}\sum_{t\in Q}\log s_t\right).
\]
Any trusted task at or below its null invalidates the headline rather than
allowing compensation by an unrelated domain. Version 0.3 therefore does not
claim aggregate coverage of function or interaction: EC and Human PPI are raw
diagnostics until their benchmarks pass reinstatement criteria. The old
six-domain v0.2 aggregate is retained only as an explicitly named legacy field.

\subsection{How a frozen embedding becomes an evaluation score}

Every downstream result in Table~\ref{tab:pcore-composition} is a linear-probe
measurement, not end-to-end fine-tuning. For sequence $x$ of $L$ valid amino
acids, the checkpoint encoder $E_\theta$ is placed in inference mode and
$\theta$ is never updated. After removing special and padding tokens, its
final-layer residue vectors are $h_i\in\mathbb{R}^d$. Deterministic windows
handle sequences longer than 2,046 residues, and sequence tasks receive
\[
  z(x)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=1}^{L}h_i,
  \qquad \widehat{y}=Wz(x)+b,
\]
followed only by the declared logistic or ridge link. Secondary structure uses
$h_i$ directly to predict one label per residue. Human PPI forms a symmetric
pair vector
$\phi(a,b)=[\lvert z(a)-z(b)\rvert;z(a)\odot z(b)]$ before binary logistic
regression. Remote homology uses multinomial logistic regression; EC and
DeepLoc2 use one-vs-rest multilabel logistic regression; FLIP2 uses ridge
regression. Thus the fitted layer has no nonlinear hidden representation and
tests what information is linearly accessible in the frozen encoder
\citep{rao2019tape,xu2022peer}.

Leakage control is procedural. A feature standardizer is fit on probe-training
examples only and then applied unchanged to validation and test. Each candidate
$C\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10\}$ or
$\alpha\in\{0.01,0.1,1,10\}$ is fit on probe train; validation selects one
candidate, and that already-fitted probe is applied once to the untouched test
split. It is not refit on validation. DeepLoc2 repeats this procedure in five
rotating folds. Reported bootstraps resample frozen test predictions by the
declared biological unit; neither encoder nor probe is refit inside a replicate.
Consequently a score is conditional on the data split, pooling rule, probe
family, regularization grid, and metric. It is evidence about accessible
representation quality, not a universal property of an embedding or a claim
about fine-tuned performance.

\subsection{Contact protocol}

The release contact score follows the 2026 ESMC definition
\citep{candido2026language,rao2021unsupervised}: all-layer/all-head attention
planes are symmetrized and passed to a frozen logistic probe fit on 20
structures. A residue pair is a contact when C$\beta$ distance is below
8\AA{} (C$\alpha$ for glycine); long range means sequence separation at least
24. For each eligible chain, precision is measured among the top $L$ predicted
pairs, and the headline is the unweighted mean over chains. Chains are cropped
to 510 residues and must contain at least $L$ true eligible contacts. Our full
manifest is built from the 2024-02-28 PDB snapshot and contains 20,775
evaluation chains. The fast diagnostic is a frozen SHA-ranked sample of 1,024
chains with a paired 5,000-replicate chain bootstrap.

\subsection{Routine diagnostic and evaluator acceleration}

The revised default speedrun diagnostic embeds only the sequences needed for
trusted remote homology and FLIP2 hydro. It stores protein means, skips
bootstrap, runs the two CPU probes concurrently with independent ten-minute
limits, and never forms a P-CORE score. Human PPI was removed from this routine
gate when quarantined. Checkpoints are evaluated on separate GPUs when
available. Component receipts are atomic and restartable.

P-CORE v0.5-alpha additionally requests only the final backbone state, omits
the unused language-model head and all-layer tuple, and packs proteins by
length under a fixed residue budget. For 6B, four deterministic contiguous
sequence shards wrote disjoint entries into one atomic store. The complete
178,819-protein pass, 666-protein residue pass, five probes, 10,000 resamples,
and reduction finished in 967.3 seconds on four A100s; the task populations and
metrics were unchanged.

The exact evaluator is accelerated without changing metrics by (i) caching one
protein mean per sequence, (ii) writing residue arrays only for secondary
structure, (iii) length-bucketing windows under a 32,768-residue budget,
(iv) running all six probes as restartable tasks with four CPU threads each,
and (v) scoring the 20,775 contact chains in three deterministic GPU shards
while P-CORE embeds on the fourth GPU. The contact merger restores global
SHA-ranked order and performs the full 5,000-replicate bootstrap. For the
four-hour checkpoint, the longest contact shard took 3,044.6 seconds and exact
P-CORE took 3,226.7 seconds, giving a 53.8-minute critical path. For the
sixteen-hour checkpoint these times were 2,705.0 and 3,132.0 seconds, giving a
52.2-minute critical path. EC alone used 2,653.1 seconds on the latter and
remains the dominant exact-probe cost. Further
simplification is conditional: one frozen regularization value
per routine task, or an SGD/ridge secondary-structure surrogate, may replace
the expensive grid/LBFGS path only after released models and archived
checkpoints demonstrate ranking preservation. Otherwise the system falls back
to the exact release evaluator rather than silently redefining it.

\section{Limitations and claim boundary}

Both completed runs are short relative to published protein-language-model
training, single-seed at each budget, and Stage 1 only. Because the training
seeds differ, their paired evaluation intervals do not isolate training duration
from stochastic initialization. Although both exact evaluations are complete,
the stronger run remains substantially below released ESMC-300M on the
selection aggregate and contact and supports a systems plus local-scaling claim,
not a model-quality promotion. Representative-only sampling still differs from
the ESMC paper; OMG/IMG is
only an open surrogate for the JGI role; local proxy learning rate and weight
decay are hypotheses; the paper/code residual scaling differs; source-license
review remains open; and EC and Human PPI are presently untrusted. These are promotion
blockers, not footnotes.

The public repository should therefore describe a research system and its
receipts, not advertise a state-of-the-art model until G6--G7 pass. Data and
checkpoint releases must preserve source terms, disclose every preprocessing
step, and include a machine-readable statement of which claims are diagnostic,
candidate-level, or release-level.
